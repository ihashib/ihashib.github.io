Rovers for Mars

BRACU Mongol Tori

BRAC University's Mars rover team · Sub-team lead · 2019 to 2021

The rover's systems backbone

Software, radio links, cameras and the base station

Mongol Tori designs and builds a Mars rover prototype that competes against university teams from around the world, driving rough terrain, operating a robotic arm and running science tasks far from its operators. I orchestrated the backbone that ties it together: the software that drives the rover and the links that connect it to the people operating it.

  • First on the rover ROS: I taught myself ROS during the COVID break and brought it to Mongol Tori for the first time as the rover's control stack, alongside a Python socket-based control system and inverse kinematics with Gazebo simulation for the 6-DOF arm.
  • Long-range link: Ubiquiti Rocket M2 radios with sector antennas and Bullet M2 radios with omni antennas.
  • Cameras: analog 5.8 GHz FPV and digital IP cameras placed around the rover for remote operation.
  • Backup: a 700 MHz communication link for when the main link drops.
  • Base station: the network for controlling and monitoring the rover.
